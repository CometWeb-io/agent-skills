# Pillar 3 - Authority and trust

Purpose: evaluate concrete trust, provenance, expertise, entity, reputation, and reference signals.
Use E-E-A-T as a quality lens, not as a single ranking factor or magic score.

## AUT-01 - Publisher/business transparency

Look for information appropriate to the site type:

- who operates the site;
- contact/support path;
- real business identity where commercial;
- editorial or review policies where relevant;
- corrections/returns/privacy/legal information when appropriate;
- physical/location facts for local businesses where relevant.

Absence of a Team page is not automatically a failure. Score whether users can identify and trust
the responsible publisher.

## AUT-02 - Authorship/provenance where useful

Authorship matters differently by content type. Check whether users can tell who created or reviewed
high-stakes or expert content and whether the stated credentials are relevant and verifiable.

For low-stakes utility/product pages, a named author may be unnecessary. Use `N/A` rather than
forcing author bios everywhere.

Person schema can improve entity clarity, but it is not itself proof of expertise.

## AUT-03 - First-hand expertise or defensible evidence

Look for:

- firsthand use/experience where the page claims it;
- methodology;
- original examples;
- citations to primary/authoritative sources;
- transparent calculations;
- disclosed limitations;
- expert review where the topic warrants it.

This is often more meaningful than decorative badges or generic claims such as "industry-leading".

## AUT-04 - Entity identity consistency

Check whether the organization, product, people, and locations are consistently identified across:

- site copy;
- Organization/Person/LocalBusiness schema when present;
- social/profile links;
- contact/location information;
- knowledge/reputation sources when available.

Avoid brittle "character-for-character NAP" rules. Normalize formatting differences and focus on
conflicting facts that could confuse users or systems.

## AUT-05 - External reputation and mentions

Use connected/web evidence to assess independent discussion and reputation when relevant.

Distinguish:

- genuine editorial/industry mentions;
- reviews and community discussion;
- citations/references to original work;
- paid/syndicated/low-quality placements;
- self-created profiles.

Secondary studies may show correlations between brand mentions, video presence, and AI visibility.
Treat those as experiment inputs, not causal requirements.

## AUT-06 - Reference/backlink quality

Use Ahrefs, Semrush, Search Console links, Bing data, or another defensible source when available.
If no dataset exists, use `NOT_ASSESSED` rather than guessing from brand size.

Evaluate relevance, independence, and quality of references rather than raw referring-domain count.

## AUT-07 - YMYL trust safeguards

Use `N/A` for non-YMYL content.

For health, finance, legal, safety, or other high-impact topics, look for stronger safeguards:

- qualified authors/reviewers where expertise is expected;
- current authoritative sources;
- clear scope and limitations;
- date/review information where facts can change;
- transparent conflicts/sponsorship;
- safe escalation to professional advice where appropriate.

Do not claim that a specific author-bio template is a direct ranking requirement. Score the trust
risk created by missing provenance or unreliable claims.
