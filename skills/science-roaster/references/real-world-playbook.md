# Real-world operational playbook

Use this guide for production scientific review involving manuscript + supplement + protocol/preregistration + code/data + reviewer responses, or when the evidence set is incomplete.

## Intake

1. Pin the manuscript/version and identify the central inferential claims.
2. Register supplement, protocol, preregistration, code/data, and reviewer-response documents as separate sources.
3. Distinguish source-of-truth materials from narrative derivatives.
4. Decide whether literature/citation verification is authorized. SOURCE_BOUND remains the default.
5. Create a review session manifest for multi-source or revision reviews.

## Evidence acquisition order

Prefer: primary manuscript data/analysis description -> protocol/preregistration -> supplement -> code/data artifacts -> reviewer response -> external literature. A response letter is evidence of an author claim, not proof that the underlying analysis is correct.

## Production failure modes

- Missing reporting is not missing procedure.
- More repeated measurements are not automatically more independent units.
- A significant p-value does not repair construct/reference validity.
- A robustness table does not rescue leakage or a changed estimand.
- A revision that changes the claim may resolve overclaiming without validating the original claim.
- External novelty verification must be separated from manuscript-internal review.

## Review budget

Prioritize reference/measurement validity, estimand and independent unit, inclusion/missingness, confirmatory/exploratory boundaries, leakage/holdout, uncertainty, multiplicity, and the central claim-to-evidence link. Do not spend the budget on formatting when the inference is unresolved.

## Closure

Use the smallest repair level that actually restores the inference. Do not demand new data when reanalysis or claim narrowing is sufficient; do not accept prose-only repair when the defect is analytic or design-level.
