# Science Roaster review rubric

Use only dimensions material to the study profile and claims.

## 1. Research question and falsifiability

- Is the question precise enough to be contradicted by data?
- Are population, construct, intervention/exposure, comparator, and endpoint recoverable?
- Does the headline claim match the question actually tested?

## 2. Novelty boundary

- What is genuinely new relative to the manuscript's cited baseline?
- Is novelty a method, dataset, validation boundary, empirical result, synthesis, or merely terminology?
- Does the paper overclaim priority or generality?

## 3. Construct and measurement validity

- What construct is intended?
- How is it operationalized?
- What does the instrument/reference actually measure?
- Are calibration, drift, repeatability, uncertainty, baseline subtraction, preprocessing, and system/time boundary described?
- Is measurement error propagated into downstream claims?

## 4. Study design and controls

- Are control/comparator conditions capable of distinguishing the proposed explanation from alternatives?
- Are assignment/exposure and analysis units aligned?
- Are order, carryover, contamination, learning, and temporal effects controlled where relevant?

## 5. Sampling and analysis population

- What population does the sample represent?
- How were units included/excluded?
- Does the analysis set differ from the enrolled/observed set?
- Are repeated, clustered, paired, or hierarchical observations treated as such?

## 6. Missingness

- How much data is missing and where?
- Is missingness concentrated by condition/device/site/group?
- Does complete-case filtering alter the estimand or population?
- Are imputation/exclusion assumptions justified?

## 7. Estimand and statistical conclusion

- What quantity is being estimated?
- Are uncertainty intervals/tests tied to that quantity?
- Are repeated measurements mistaken for independent replication?
- Are descriptive spread metrics described as uncertainty of an estimator when they are not?

## 8. Multiplicity and researcher degrees of freedom

- Are multiple endpoints/subgroups/models/thresholds distinguished from a single confirmatory family?
- Are post-hoc choices labeled honestly?
- Could model/analysis selection have used the same data later presented as validation?

## 9. Predictive/model development integrity

- Is there leakage across train/calibration/validation/holdout?
- Is the holdout genuinely unseen until model/procedure freeze?
- Are comparators strong enough to show value over trivial baselines?
- Are calibration and error distributions reported, not only aggregate performance?

## 10. Causal and mechanistic language

- Does design support causal identification?
- Are confounders/alternative explanations addressed?
- Is mechanism directly tested or merely narrated after association?

## 11. Robustness and sensitivity

- Do reasonable analytic choices materially change the conclusion?
- Are negative controls, ablations, alternate specifications, or comparator analyses informative?
- Is robustness selectively reported?

## 12. External validity and transport

- Which populations/devices/settings/time periods were actually tested?
- Are device/site/cohort differences confounded with other factors?
- Does the discussion generalize beyond observed evidence?

## 13. Reproducibility and provenance

- Are data/code/protocol/environment/seeds/versions available or sufficiently described?
- Are transformations and exclusion rules reconstructable?
- Is a frozen analysis artifact or provenance chain available where needed?

## 14. Figures and tables

- Do axes, denominators, uncertainty, and sample sizes support the visual inference?
- Does visual emphasis invite comparisons the text later says are invalid?
- Are exploratory results visually promoted above confirmatory ones?

## 15. Discussion and limitations

- Do limitations materially narrow the headline claim?
- Are negative/null results represented rather than hidden behind narrative emphasis?
- Does the conclusion match the strongest surviving evidence?

## 16. Ethics, governance, and conflicts

Inspect only where relevant to the research domain. Distinguish missing reporting from demonstrated noncompliance. Do not speculate about author motives or conflicts beyond disclosed evidence.
