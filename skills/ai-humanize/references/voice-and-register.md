# Voice and register

Humanization should preserve authorship cues that are intentional, not replace them with a universal house style.

## Style-reference hierarchy

Use this order when signals conflict:

1. explicit user instructions;
2. destination conventions;
3. dedicated style samples supplied by the user;
4. stable voice cues in the source;
5. general natural-writing defaults.

A style sample is not a factual source. Do not import its people, events, opinions, numbers, claims, anecdotes, or examples into the target text.

## Voice anchors

When enough source or style-reference text exists, infer only stable traits such as:

- direct vs reflective;
- terse vs expansive;
- formal vs conversational;
- preferred sentence complexity;
- contraction use;
- heading density;
- punctuation habits;
- degree of humor or irony;
- use of first person;
- tolerance for fragments;
- technical vocabulary density;
- how explicitly transitions are signposted.

Preserve these traits unless they are the problem the user asked to fix.

Do not copy distinctive phrases from a style reference unless they are ordinary language or the user explicitly asked for that wording.

## Over-editing brake

Do not change a sentence merely because a different sentence is possible.

Preserve wording when it is:

- clear;
- specific;
- idiomatic for the author/register;
- factually precise;
- rhythmically useful;
- a deliberate repeated motif;
- the correct technical/legal term.

Prefer deletion of empty framing over replacement with new filler.

## Avoid a new humanizer fingerprint

Do not enforce universal patterns such as:

- always-short paragraphs;
- mandatory fragments;
- a fixed mix of long and short sentences;
- obligatory rhetorical questions;
- one-line punch endings;
- a required amount of slang;
- forced contractions;
- deliberate typos or grammar mistakes;
- a ban on all em dashes, colons, semicolons, or formal transitions.

The destination and author determine rhythm.

## Register notes

### Documentation

Clarity and scanability dominate personality. Repetition of exact names is often better than creative synonymy.

### Academic/scientific

Conventional phrases can be legitimate. Do not casualize away uncertainty or technical precision.

### Legal/compliance

Normative wording, defined terms, exceptions, and scope dominate stylistic variety.

### Marketing

Specificity should come from supplied evidence, not invented proof points. Remove empty hype before adding new language.

### Social/personal

A sharper voice is fine, but do not manufacture lived experience, first-person history, or emotional claims absent from the source/user.
