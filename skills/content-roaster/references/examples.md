# Content Roaster examples

## Strong finding

**Anchor:** `"AI-powered platform for modern teams"` in hero.

**Observation:** the phrase names neither the team type nor an observable mechanism.

**Failure mode:** category-generic promise.

**Why it matters:** a cold visitor cannot distinguish the offer from unrelated AI SaaS.

**Repair:** name the target team, job, and mechanism.

**Acceptance:** after reading hero only, a cold reader can describe who it is for and what changes.

## Bad finding

> This copy is boring and obviously AI-written.

Why it fails: no anchor, no evidence state, no reader consequence, no repair, and an unsupported authorship claim.

## Withdrawn finding

Initial concern: pricing has no risk reversal. Falsifier pass finds a 30-day refund guarantee in the FAQ. Remove the finding instead of keeping a sarcastic version of a disproven criticism.

## Omission discipline

If only the hero was supplied, do not claim the page lacks case studies. Record `not_inspected` or a verification question. Use `MISSING` only when the reviewed scope is sufficient for the omission.

## Root-cause discipline

If the hero, CTA, and FAQ all repeat a vague promise because the positioning itself is undefined, do not emit three independent MAJOR findings. Create one root cause and link the local symptoms to it.

## DELTA review

Prior finding key: `hero-generic-promise`. The revised hero names the agency audience but still omits the mechanism. Mark the prior finding `PARTIAL`, not `RESOLVED`, because the original acceptance condition still fails.

## Challenger / Defender / Arbiter example

**Challenger:** the pricing page asks for a paid pilot before establishing why the outcome claim is credible.

**Defender search:** inspect proof blocks, FAQ, guarantee, case studies, plan notes, and the sequence before the first paid CTA for evidence that earns the commitment.

**Arbiter:** if credible proof appears before the CTA, downgrade or withdraw. If proof is absent or mismatched and the requested commitment is high cost, keep the finding with the narrower decision-stage consequence.

The final report records the surviving claim, not the reviewer's first impression.
